# Worked Example: The Empty Quadrant Was the Point

**Synthetic teaching case.** All figures, companies, and sources below are invented for teaching. Nothing here is a claim about a real organization.

**Current market boundary:** Mid-market (50-999 employees) revenue operations buyers, United States and Canada.
**Current product boundary:** Reconciliation tooling with certified audit lineage and two native ERP connectors.
**Decision supported:** Where next fiscal year's investment goes.
**Evidence base:** Fusion brief 2026-08-18, five forces read 2026-08-18, GEOINT sweep 2026-08-18.

## The Penetration Check

| Test | Figure | Source | Read |
|---|---|---|---|
| Customers held / addressable denominator | 340 of 38,400 US establishments in band | GEOINT sweep, vintage 2024 | **0.9%.** Barely started. |
| Win rate in the served segment | 31% of evaluated deals | Our CRM, trailing 4 quarters | Healthy; we win when we compete |
| Share against non-consumption | Low single digits of the 60-80% solved by spreadsheet | Landscape scan, Inference | Effectively untouched |

**Verdict: penetration is nowhere near exhausted.** We hold under one percent of the population we already know how to sell to, and win nearly a third of the deals we contest.

This check took twenty minutes and it settled an argument that had been running for two quarters. The room's working assumption was that the mid-market was "getting crowded," which was a statement about the number of vendors, not about the number of unsold buyers.

## 1. Market Penetration — existing product, existing market

| Move | Evidence | Source | Risk | The unknown driving that rating |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Named-account campaign to Cartelane's former sub-$119 customers | They removed the entry tier; floor rose 144% this quarter | SIGINT capture, 2026-08-18 vs 2026-05-14 | **Low** | Whether those customers absorbed the increase or are shopping. Testable with outbound in six weeks. |
| Cut onboarding from six weeks to four | Implementation speed is an RFP criterion in 4 of 4 observed RFPs; we lose it to a competitor at three weeks | Snapshot + win/loss, 2026 | **Low** | Whether four weeks is enough to stop losing the criterion, or whether three is the real bar |
| Displacement play against partner-built connector users | Cartelane closed its partner program; those customers are stranded | TECHINT deprecation notice, 2026-07-01 | **Medium** | How many such customers exist; the partner directory shows 31 partners but not their account counts |

Three evidenced moves, all low or medium risk, all in the quadrant nobody wanted to talk about.

## 2. Market Development — existing product, new market

| Move | Evidence | Source | Risk | The unknown driving that rating |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Germany entry | 6,900 eligible enterprises; Cartelane hiring EMEA solutions architects | GEOINT sweep vintage 2023; HUMINT 2026-08 | **High** | **The buyer title does not exist.** No German statistical system classifies revenue operations as an occupation; the duties sit across Controlling and sales leadership. We do not know who to sell to. |
| UK entry | Shared language, similar firmographics, existing inbound interest | Inbound records, 2026 | **Medium** | Whether our audit-lineage story maps onto UK audit expectations, which differ |

The Germany risk rating is high for one specific, nameable reason — and naming it converts an expensive market entry into a cheap research question. Ten buyer interviews would resolve it for the cost of a week.

## 3. Product Development — new product, existing market

| Move | Evidence | Source | Risk | The unknown driving that rating |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| "Which system is wrong" disambiguation | Recurring across two independent sources in VoC; it is the job underneath all three top feature requests | VoC mining, 2026-08 | **Medium** | Whether it decides purchases or is post-purchase frustration. Reviews come from people who already bought. |
| Third and fourth ERP connectors | 14 of 22 competitor postings name two specific ERPs | HUMINT, 2026-08-15 | **Medium-High** | Whether we can win a connector race against a team of 22 from a base of two. The forces read says differentiation is falling here. |

## 4. Diversification — new product, new market

> **No evidence found.** Nothing in the evidence base supports a diversification move. Sources checked: fusion brief, forces read, landscape scan, GEOINT sweep, VoC mining. No adjacent market shows both an unserved job we could serve and a product we could credibly build.

The quadrant is empty and stays empty. There was a proposal in circulation — a compliance-reporting product for regulated healthcare, a new product in a new market — and it has no evidence behind it in this document. Writing it into the box would have made it look assessed. It is addressed below instead.

## Recommended Sequence

1. **Penetration: onboarding to four weeks + the Cartelane price-refugee campaign** — Q1-Q2. Produces revenue against a known cost of sale and removes the RFP criterion we currently lose. Funds everything below.
2. **Product development: the disambiguation capability** — Q2-Q3. **Depends on** the win/loss round in Q1 confirming that "which system is wrong" affects purchase rather than satisfaction. Also depends on penetration revenue, because this is the quarter with no new bookings attached to it.
3. **Market development: UK** — Q3-Q4. **Depends on** the disambiguation capability shipping, because the UK inbound interest is concentrated among buyers who cite exactly that gap. Entering first and building second would burn the inbound.
4. **Market development: Germany** — deferred pending research. **Depends on** ten buyer interviews resolving who actually holds the reconciliation job. That research can run in parallel from Q2 at negligible cost.

| Dependency | Why |
|---|---|
| Penetration before product development | Funding, and the Q1 win/loss round is the gate on move 2 |
| Disambiguation before UK | The UK inbound is concentrated on that specific gap; entering without it wastes warm demand |
| German research before German entry | The buyer title is unestablished; a country entry aimed at a role that may not exist is the most expensive way to find that out |

## Not Yet

- **The tempting move:** the compliance-reporting product for regulated healthcare — a diversification play with an executive sponsor.
- **Why the evidence says wait:** it is new product *and* new market simultaneously, we hold 0.9% of the market we already understand, and nothing in five sweeps produced a single signal about healthcare reconciliation buyers. The five forces read also says our durable position is audit lineage and liability — which this move would abandon rather than extend.
- **What would change that:** a regulator making reconciliation lineage mandatory in a healthcare context, or ten interviews with healthcare finance buyers producing a job we could serve. Either is cheap to pursue and neither has been.

Writing this section is what stops the proposal returning at the next planning meeting as though it had never been assessed.

## The Assumption That Breaks This Sequence

- **The assumption:** that penetration remains open — that the 0.9% figure reflects an untapped market rather than a market that has already decided it does not want this category.
- **How we would notice:** win rate in the served segment falling below roughly 20% over two quarters, or the non-consumption share holding steady while our outbound volume rises. `mi-watch-competitors` monitors the competitive half; our own CRM monitors the rest.
- **What we would do instead:** if penetration is closing rather than open, the sequence inverts — UK and the disambiguation capability move ahead of the domestic campaign, because the constraint is demand rather than reach.

## Final Step

1. Commit to the sequence, starting with onboarding and the price-refugee campaign (Recommended)
2. Fund the ten German buyer interviews now, in parallel and cheaply
3. Set a quarterly review against the penetration assumption
4. Size the UK move before Q3
